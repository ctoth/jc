\begin{abstract}
Adjamagbo's separable Jacobian conjecture is now known to fail in
characteristic~$2$: in dimension three by Huq-Kuruvilla (July 23, 2026)
and in dimension two by Mondello (July 29, 2026). We report two
findings about the \emph{landscape} of such counterexamples. First, a
parity obstruction in normal form: in two exhaustively classified
strata of identity-plus-homogeneous maps over $\F_2$ --- all $262{,}144$
maps with quadratic higher part in dimension $3$ and all $16{,}384$
plane maps of degree $\le 3$ --- every map with $\det JF = 1$ has
generic degree $1$ or even, so no counterexample of odd degree exists
there; a third, $16.7$-million-map cubic-homogeneous stratum agrees on
all $9{,}813$ of its $10{,}144$ unit-Jacobian maps classified so far.
General maps --- the known counterexamples included --- violate the
pattern. If this parity law holds
for identity-plus-$H$ form in general, no characteristic-$2$ analogue of
the Bass--Connell--Wright reduction can preserve counterexamples.
Second, a search method: compiling $\det JF = 1$ over unknown $\F_2$
coefficients into CNF (algebraic normal form plus Tseitin encoding) and
enumerating all solutions with a SAT solver. Transferring the monomial
supports of the Alp\"oge--Fable characteristic-$0$ counterexample mod
$2$ proved decisive: the support-mirrored $2^{58}$-map family produced a
new degree-$3$ counterexample within $3{,}000$ enumerated unit-Jacobian
maps ($8{,}277$ candidates with the tame census signature among
$6.7$ million members enumerated), while a generic low-degree
$z$-linear family of $2^{45}$ maps produced $9.8$ million unit-Jacobian
maps and not a single one. The
resulting map --- target-shear-equivalent to
$(z + xy(1{+}y{+}xy)(1{+}xz),\, y(1{+}xy),\, x(1{+}xz))$ --- agrees with
Huq-Kuruvilla's on every invariant we computed yet is provably not
equivalent to it via short words in affine maps and elementary monomial
shears; a Lean~4 kernel certificate (no axioms, no \texttt{sorry})
verifies its determinant identity and witness collision.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and timeline}

The Jacobian conjecture of Keller asserts that a polynomial map
$F\colon\A^n \to \A^n$ over a field of characteristic $0$ with
$\det JF \in k^\times$ is invertible. It was disproved in dimension~$3$
in July 2026 by Alp\"oge and Fable \cite{alpoge,sbs,tao}, with
extensions to all dimensions greater than two \cite{dims}. In
characteristic $p$ the naive statement fails trivially
($x \mapsto x - x^p$); the standard repair, due to Adjamagbo
\cite{adjamagbo}, adds the hypothesis that $p$ does not divide
$[k(x_1,\dots,x_n) : k(F_1,\dots,F_n)]$. Reduction of the
Alp\"oge--Fable map modulo primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ refutes this
\emph{separable Jacobian conjecture} for $p \ge 5$; its determinant
$-2$ vanishes mod $2$, leaving characteristic $2$ open --- briefly. On
July 23, Huq-Kuruvilla gave an explicit three-variable counterexample
\cite{hk}; on July 29, Mondello gave a \emph{plane} counterexample
derived from a coordinate permutation of that construction, with a
Lean~4 formalization \cite{mondello}. We have verified both maps
computationally with the tooling of this paper (unit determinant,
generic degree $3$, and the stated collisions).

This note contributes to the emerging landscape rather than to the
existence question. The organizing observation: \emph{all three known
characteristic-$2$ counterexamples --- Huq-Kuruvilla's, Mondello's, and
the one found here --- have odd generic degree $3$ and live outside
identity-plus-$H$ normal form, while in every normal-form stratum we
have exhaustively classified, odd degree $\ge 3$ never occurs}; and the
two three-variable maps, though provably not related by low-complexity
automorphisms, have fiber-drop loci with identical fine structure.
Three independent witnesses thus separate the parity question from
being a pattern in one dataset. We present: the parity obstruction and
the strata classifications (Section~2), a SAT-based search method with
a sharp positive/negative contrast (Section~3), the new example and its
verification (Section~4), and the equivalence-resistance analysis
(Section~5).

\section{The parity obstruction}

Call a polynomial self-map of $\A^n_{\F_2}$ of \emph{identity-plus-$H$
form} if $F = \mathrm{id} + H$ with every monomial of $H$ of degree
$\ge 2$ --- the shape of the cubic-homogeneous Bass--Connell--Wright
normal form \cite{bcw} (we claim no characteristic-$2$ reduction
theorem).

\begin{theorem}[exhaustive classifications]
\label{thm:parity}
Over $\F_2$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Of the $262{,}144$ maps of $\A^3$ with $H$ quadratic, exactly
$4096$ have $\det JF = 1$; their generic degrees are
$1$ ($176$), $2$ ($728$), $4$ ($1176$), $6$ ($672$), $8$ ($1344$).
\item Of the $16{,}384$ maps of $\A^2$ with $H$ spanned by monomials of
degree $2$ and $3$, exactly $160$ have $\det JF = 1$; their generic
degrees are $1$ ($10$), $2$ ($54$), $4$ ($48$), $6$ ($48$).
\end{enumerate}
In particular no map in either stratum has odd generic degree $\ge 3$.
\end{theorem}

Two slices admit proofs rather than enumeration. In dimension $1$,
$f' = 1$ forces $f = x + g(x^2)$, of degree $1$ or even. In the plane
quadratic-only stratum, characteristic $2$ kills every square's
derivative, so $\det JF = 1 + b y + e x$ where $b, e$ are the
$xy$-coefficients: a unit iff both vanish, leaving exactly the
Frobenius-affine maps $v \mapsto v + M v^{(2)}$ --- additive group
homomorphisms whose derivative is the identity, hence \'etale with
finite kernel and dominant (so the generic degree is defined), of
generic degree equal to the kernel size, a power of $2$. In the three-dimensional quadratic stratum the determinant
conditions solve completely: the $4096$ maps are exactly
$F = \mathrm{id} + \bigl(d(y{+}ex)(z{+}fx),\; e(x{+}dy)(z{+}fy),\;
f(x{+}dz)(y{+}ez)\bigr) + (\text{Frobenius part})$ for
$(d,e,f) \in \F_2^3$, i.e.\ $8 \times 2^9$.

A third, much larger stratum is nearly complete at the time of writing:
$\det JF = 1$ for cubic-homogeneous $H$ on $\A^3$ forces the three
graded pieces $e_1 = e_2 = e_3 = 0$ separately; the $e_1$-kernel
contains $16{,}777{,}216$ maps, of which $10{,}144$ have unit Jacobian,
and $9{,}813$ of these are exactly classified so far with degrees in
$\{1, 2, 4, 6, 10\}$ --- again no odd degree $\ge 3$; the remainder are
maps whose function-field Gr\"obner bases are computationally hard and
are being finished with permuted-order methods. The final histogram
will appear in the repository \cite{repo} and a revision.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Question (parity).} Does every map of identity-plus-$H$ form
over a field of characteristic $2$ with $\det JF = 1$ have generic
degree $1$ or even?
\end{quote}

The known counterexamples \cite{hk,mondello}, and ours below, all have
odd degree $3$ and all live \emph{outside} identity-plus-$H$ form. If
the answer to the question is yes, then no characteristic-$2$ analogue
of the Bass--Connell--Wright reduction --- which in characteristic $0$
transports any counterexample into cubic-homogeneous
identity-plus-$H$ form in more variables --- can preserve
counterexamples, and the obstruction would be worth locating precisely
in the reduction (compare \cite{vde} for where characteristic $0$
enters). Either resolution seems interesting; $4 \times 10^5$ random
identity-plus-$H$ samples beyond the exhaustive strata also satisfy
parity.

\section{The SAT search}

The condition $\det JF = 1$, for $F$ with \emph{unknown} $\F_2$
coefficients on fixed monomial supports, is a system of Boolean
equations: each coefficient of $\det JF - 1$ is an XOR of ANDs of the
unknown bits (algebraic normal form). Tseitin encoding turns the
system into CNF; CaDiCaL then \emph{enumerates all} unit-Jacobian
members of a family under blocking clauses. Two refinements matter:
bits appearing in no determinant condition (pure Frobenius directions,
e.g.\ $x^2$-coefficients of the non-$z$ parts) are pinned to zero in
the solver and swept combinatorially afterwards, a $64$-fold saving;
and enumerated maps are triaged by exhaustive fiber censuses over
$\F_4$ and $\F_8$ --- a map earns an exact (function-field Gr\"obner)
degree computation only if its $\F_8$ census shows fiber sizes in
$\{1,3\}$ exclusively, the signature a separable degree-$3$ map must
show and wild maps essentially never do.

The search heuristic that worked is \emph{monomial support transfer}:
take the supports of the characteristic-$0$ counterexample's components
mod $2$, allow all $\F_2$ coefficients on those supports (plus small
neighborhoods), and let SAT enumerate. The contrast is sharp:

\begin{itemize}
\item Support-mirrored $z$-linear family ($2^{58}$ maps): the
counterexample of Section~4 appeared within the first $3{,}000$
enumerated unit-Jacobian maps; by the end of the run, $8{,}277$ of
$6.7$ million enumerated members carried the tame census signature
(their exact classification is ongoing; the first six were all variants
of the single base map of Section~4).
\item Generic low-degree $z$-linear family ($2^{45}$ maps,
$P,R,T$ of degree $\le 2$, $Q,S,U$ of degree $\le 3$):
$9.8$ million unit-Jacobian maps enumerated, \emph{zero} passed the
census sieve.
\end{itemize}

The structure of the characteristic-$0$ example, not mere
$z$-linearity, carried the information.

\section{The witness map}

\begin{theorem}
Over any field $k$ of characteristic $2$, the map
\begin{align*}
F_1 &= z + xy + xy^2 + x^2y^2 + x^2yz + x^2y^2z + x^3y^2z,\\
F_2 &= y + xy^2,\\
F_3 &= x + y + xy^2 + x^2z
\end{align*}
satisfies $\det JF = 1$ identically, has generic degree
$[k(x,y,z):k(F)] = 3$, and is not injective:
$F(0,0,1) = F(1,0,1) = F(1,1,1) = (1,0,0)$.
\end{theorem}

Degree $3$ forces separability (the inseparable degree is a power of
$2$ dividing the total degree), so Adjamagbo's hypothesis holds. We
make no claim about tame ramification at valuations at infinity: $F$ is
a nonproper generically \'etale map, not a finite covering. Composing
with the target shear $(a,b,c) \mapsto (a,b,c+b)$ yields the normal
form
\[
\bigl(\, z + xy\,(1+y+xy)(1+xz),\;\; y\,(1+xy),\;\; x\,(1+xz) \,\bigr),
\]
visibly in the $(1+\cdot)$-unit grammar of the characteristic-$0$
construction.

Checks: on $\F_2$-points $a^k = a$, so $F$ collapses to
$(z+xy+xyz,\, y+xy,\, x+y+xy+xz)$ and the collision is a hand
computation. The determinant identity and the collision are certified
by a Lean~4 kernel certificate (Lean core only: no imports, no axioms,
no \texttt{sorry}; ancillary file \texttt{JcChar2.lean}); a collision
of rational points persists over every extension. The degree is the
staircase count of a Gr\"obner basis over the function field
$\F_2(A,B,C)$, corroborated (not independently proved) by exhaustive
censuses over $\F_8$--$\F_{64}$ with every fiber of size $1$ or $3$,
and by the $x$-eliminant specialization at $(0,1,0)$ whose non-artifact
factor is the irreducible separable cubic $x^3+x+1$. The fiber over
$(1,0,0)$ has Gr\"obner basis $\{x^2{+}x,\, xy{+}y,\, y^2{+}y,\,
z{+}1\}$: the geometric fiber has length $3$ and is the disjoint union
of the three claimed reduced rational points.

\section{Equivalence resistance}

Is this map equivalent to Huq-Kuruvilla's
$(x + x^2y,\, y + xz + x^2yz,\, z + x^2z^2)$ under composition with
automorphisms? Every invariant we computed agrees: fiber-size censuses
are identical over $\F_4, \F_8, \F_{16}, \F_{32}$ (both maps have $S_3$
monodromy with split-fiber fraction approaching the Chebotarev value
$1/6$, so censuses likely cannot separate any two such maps); the
stratification of targets by \emph{geometric} fiber size --- computed
per rational target by Gr\"obner staircase after adjoining a primitive
element --- is identical over $\F_2, \F_4, \F_8$, and strikingly clean:
for both maps the fiber-drop locus has exactly $q^2$ rational points,
of which $q-1$ have empty fiber and the rest singletons. Yet writing
$G$ for the $1344$ invertible affine maps of $\A^3$ over $\F_2$
together with the $27$ elementary monomial shears
$x_i \mapsto x_i + m$ ($\deg m \le 3$), a complete meet-in-the-middle
search shows no $\beta \circ H \circ \alpha$ with $\alpha, \beta$ words
of length $\le 2$ over $G$ equals our normal form. The two maps agree
on everything we can measure while resisting every low-complexity
equivalence; whether a higher-complexity equivalence exists is open,
and the shared fine structure of the drop loci suggests both maps may
be coordinate presentations of one geometric mechanism --- in
characteristic $2$, where the projective-duality account of the
characteristic-$0$ example \cite{sbs} degenerates, identifying that
mechanism seems to us the interesting follow-up, now with three data
points \cite{hk,mondello}.
