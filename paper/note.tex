\title[A second characteristic-2 counterexample, by SAT search]{A second
counterexample to the separable Jacobian conjecture in
characteristic~2, found by SAT search}

\begin{abstract}
Huq-Kuruvilla recently gave an explicit polynomial endomorphism of
$\A^3$ over a field of characteristic $2$ with Jacobian determinant $1$
and function-field degree $3$ that is not injective, refuting
Adjamagbo's separable Jacobian conjecture in characteristic $2$
\cite{hk}. We present a second, independently discovered counterexample
with the same invariants, found by compiling the unit-Jacobian condition
over unknown $\F_2$ coefficients to CNF and enumerating solutions with a
SAT solver over a family mirroring the mod-$2$ monomial supports of the
Alp\"oge--Fable characteristic-$0$ counterexample. We supply a Lean~4
kernel certificate of the determinant identity and the witness collision
(no axioms, no \texttt{sorry}, no imports), a scheme-theoretic fiber
certificate showing the collision is the entire fiber, and computational
evidence for a parity phenomenon: in exhaustively classified strata of
identity-plus-homogeneous form, every unit-Jacobian map over $\F_2$ has
generic degree $1$ or even, so counterexamples appear to require leaving
that normal form.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement}

The Jacobian conjecture of Keller asserts that a polynomial map
$F\colon\A^n \to \A^n$ over a field of characteristic $0$ with
$\det JF \in k^\times$ is invertible; it was disproved in dimension $3$
by Alp\"oge and Fable in July 2026 \cite{alpoge,sbs,tao}, with
extensions to all dimensions greater than two in \cite{dims}. In
characteristic $p$ the naive statement fails trivially
($x \mapsto x - x^p$), and the standard repair, due to Adjamagbo
\cite{adjamagbo}, adds the hypothesis that $p$ does not divide the
degree $[k(x_1,\dots,x_n) : k(F_1,\dots,F_n)]$. Reduction of the
Alp\"oge--Fable map modulo primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ refutes this
\emph{separable Jacobian conjecture} in characteristic $p \ge 5$; its
determinant $-2$ vanishes mod $2$. The characteristic-$2$ case was
posed in the discussion of \cite{sbs} and settled in the negative by
Huq-Kuruvilla on July 23, 2026 \cite{hk}, via the map
$(x + x^2y,\; y + xz + x^2yz,\; z + x^2z^2)$. The present note records
a second example, found independently by the search described in
Section~2 (we learned of \cite{hk} after the fact), together with
verification artifacts of possibly independent interest.

\begin{theorem}
Over any field $k$ of characteristic $2$, let $F = (F_1,F_2,F_3)\colon
\A^3 \to \A^3$ be
\begin{align*}
F_1 &= z + xy + xy^2 + x^2y^2 + x^2yz + x^2y^2z + x^3y^2z,\\
F_2 &= y + xy^2 \;=\; y(1+xy),\\
F_3 &= x + y + xy^2 + x^2z.
\end{align*}
Then $\det JF = 1$ identically, the generic degree
$[k(x,y,z):k(F_1,F_2,F_3)]$ equals $3$, and $F$ is not injective:
\[
F(0,0,1) \;=\; F(1,0,1) \;=\; F(1,1,1) \;=\; (1,0,0).
\]
\end{theorem}

Since the extension degree is $3$ and the inseparable degree of a
finite extension in characteristic $2$ is a power of $2$ dividing the
total degree, the extension is separable and Adjamagbo's prime-to-$p$
hypothesis holds. We make no claim about tame ramification at
valuations at infinity: $F$ is a nonproper generically \'etale map, not
a finite covering, and odd total degree alone does not imply tameness
of every branch at infinity. (It is the unit Jacobian that rules out
ramification at affine points; the classical wild failures such as
$x \mapsto x + x^2$ are excluded here by the degree hypothesis, not by
any ramification claim.)

Every claim in the theorem is finite and checkable; we indicate the
checks.

\emph{The collision, by hand.} On $\F_2$-points $a^k = a$ for
$k \ge 1$, so $F$ collapses to
$(z + xy + xyz,\; y + xy,\; x + y + xy + xz)$; evaluating at the three
points takes a minute.

\emph{The determinant.} $\det JF$, expanded over $\F_2$, is $1$. This
is verified in the repository \cite{repo} by three implementations,
including a Lean~4 kernel certificate
(\texttt{lean\_export/JcChar2.lean}: Lean core only, no imports, no
axioms, no \texttt{sorry}, with an in-file polynomial algebra and
formal derivative, all arithmetic decided by the kernel). The
certificate also verifies the collision; a collision of rational points
persists over every extension field.

\emph{The degree.} A Gr\"obner basis of
$(F_1 - A,\, F_2 - B,\, F_3 - C)$ over the function field
$\F_2(A,B,C)$ has staircase of cardinality $3$; this is the proof.
Corroboration (not independent proof, since points can escape to
infinity under specialization): exhaustive fiber censuses over
$\F_8, \F_{16}, \F_{32}, \F_{64}$ show every rational fiber has
cardinality $1$ or $3$, jointly accounting for every point of the
space; and the $x$-eliminant specialized at the target $(0,1,0)$ has
non-artifact factor $x^3 + x + 1$, irreducible and separable over
$\F_2$, witnessing a degree-$3$ part.

\emph{The fiber is the collision.} Over the target $(1,0,0)$ the fiber
ideal has Gr\"obner basis $\{x^2+x,\; xy+y,\; y^2+y,\; z+1\}$. The
geometric fiber has length $3$ and is the disjoint union of the three
reduced $\F_2$-rational points listed in the theorem: no multiplicity,
no points in extension fields.

\section{How the map was found}

An investigation of unit-Jacobian maps over $\F_2$ in strata modeled on
the cubic-homogeneous Bass--Connell--Wright normal form \cite{bcw}
(identity plus homogeneous higher part; we do not claim a
characteristic-$2$ reduction theorem) produced exhaustive
classifications of two strata --- all $262{,}144$ maps with quadratic
higher part in dimension $3$, and all $16{,}384$ plane maps with higher
part of degree $\le 3$ --- in which \emph{every} unit-Jacobian map has
generic degree $1$ or even. This suggested that counterexamples require
leaving the identity-plus-$H$ shape, and the characteristic-$0$
counterexample is $z$-linear, $F_i = P_i(x,y)\,z + Q_i(x,y)$, so the
search moved there. The condition $\det JF = 1$ over \emph{unknown}
$\F_2$ coefficients on fixed monomial supports is a system of
XOR-of-AND (algebraic normal form) equations, hence a SAT instance.
With CaDiCaL enumerating models under blocking clauses, every
unit-Jacobian member of the $2^{58}$-map family whose supports mirror
the mod-$2$ monomials of the Alp\"oge--Fable map was enumerated and
sieved by fiber census for the pure $\{1,3\}$ signature; the map above
surfaced within the first $3{,}000$ models. The identical sweep of a
lower-degree $z$-linear family ($2^{45}$ maps; over $600{,}000$
unit-Jacobian members enumerated) found nothing: the mirrored support
structure mattered. We have not determined whether the present map is
equivalent to the map of \cite{hk} under composition with
automorphisms; their monomial supports differ.

\section{Remarks}

\emph{A parity question.} Does every unit-Jacobian map of
identity-plus-higher-degree form over $\F_2$ have generic degree $1$ or
even? The exhaustive strata above, a $16.7$-million-map
cubic-homogeneous computation in progress, and $4 \times 10^5$ random
samples all say yes; the theorem says the answer is no for general
maps. If the answer for identity-plus-$H$ form is yes, then no
characteristic-$2$ analogue of the Bass--Connell--Wright reduction can
carry counterexamples into that form --- worth comparing with
\cite{vde} for where characteristic $0$ enters the classical
reduction. Either resolution would be interesting.

\emph{The geometric mechanism.} The known geometric account of the
characteristic-$0$ counterexample runs through projective duality
\cite{sbs}. Characteristic $2$ is exactly where that story degenerates:
strange conics, possibly inseparable Gauss maps, failure of biduality.
Whether the present map (or that of \cite{hk}) runs a transported
version of the same mechanism or something genuinely different is, we
think, the interesting follow-up. The component $F_2 = y(1+xy)$
visibly echoes the unit structure of the original's second component,
but the search re-chose every coefficient, so this is suggestive, not
established.

\emph{Reproducibility.} All computations, the SAT machinery, the test
suite (both Lean-kernel certificates gated by corrupted-certificate
negative controls), and the scripts that found and verified the map are
at \cite{repo}.
